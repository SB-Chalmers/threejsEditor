HEATH WEB TOOLSTART WITH CLIMATE

Start with climate, then test a design.

HEATH is a course workspace for making building-performance questions concrete. Begin with a Swedish climate, make a simple model, and use evidence to discuss the consequences of a choice.

  • Choose a Swedish city or upload an EPW weather file.
  • Read the climate through temperature, humidity, degree days and wind.
  • Frame a course question about orientation, comfort, daylight or energy before modelling.

MODELDRAW BUILDING

Build a simple model you can explain.

For a course exercise, start with a deliberately simple mass. Place the footprint, state the assumptions, and keep the model readable enough to discuss with others.

  • Place footprint vertices and close the outline by returning to the first point or double-clicking.
  • Set new-building defaults for floors, floor height and colour.
  • Return to the model by selecting a building in the scene and refining the assumption.

EDITBUILDING INSPECTOR

Make the assumptions visible.

A model is useful in teaching only when its inputs are clear. HEATH keeps geometry, façade, construction, programme and systems together so students can connect a result to the choices behind it.

  • Change massing with floors, floor height and editable footprint vertices.
  • Adjust the façade with window-to-wall ratio and optional extra overhang depth.
  • Record the assumptions for constructions, programme, structural system and HVAC.

EXPLORESUN POSITION

Test a moment. Discuss the consequence.

Set a date and time, then use the scene to discuss how massing and surrounding buildings shape light and shadow. It is a prompt for observation—not a final solar-performance score.

  • Set a date for the design moment you want to inspect.
  • Move through the day in fifteen-minute increments or jump to a common time.
  • Read elevation and azimuth alongside a qualitative shadow study.

RUN STUDIESDAYLIGHT + ENERGY

Run a study when you have a question.

Name the option and run daylight and energy studies in parallel. The results help students compare the effects of their assumptions; they do not replace architectural judgement.

  • Daylight results include daylight factor, sDA and sensor-grid visualisation.
  • Energy results include heating, cooling and total energy demand.
  • Embodied GWP connects construction choices and element areas to a comparable indicator.

COMPAREDESIGN HISTORY

Compare, explain, revise.

HEATH keeps each saved option in the design history. Compare what changed, explain the outcome to your peers, and reinstate an earlier option to test the next idea.

  • Compare status-aware metrics: sDA, total energy and GWP remain unavailable until their studies finish.
  • Select a node to inspect the design and its study details.
  • Reinstate a configuration to revisit a decision rather than losing it.
